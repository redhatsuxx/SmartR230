Install Instructions
Fitting the SmartR230 emulator behind the rear SAM
← Back to home

Overview

This guide is the written companion to our installation walkthrough video and covers fitting the SmartR230 emulator behind the rear SAM. It assumes the original BCM has already been removed as part of your replacement, leaving a spare connector at the CAN bus strip that previously fed it.

The install uses a fuse-tap method for power, so you don't need to cut into any of the car's original wiring. Everything is reversible.

Before you start

Trim clips get brittle in cold weather, so this job is easier in mild conditions. Work carefully around the airbag wiring loom near the rear SAM, and avoid yanking on connectors.

Tools Required

T15 Torx
Rear console and side panel screws
T20 Torx
Side panel and trim screws
10mm socket or spanner
Earth connection
4mm Allen key
Loosens the rear SAM housing bolts so the unit can be moved

Part 1: Accessing the Rear SAM

Step 1 - Rear center console

  1. Remove the two T15 screws inside the rear center console.
  2. Lift the console section out.

Step 2 - Clear the way

  1. Slide the driver's seat fully forward.
  2. Lift the door/sill seal off - start from the back end, since the front has a hook that tucks under the carpet and can tear it if pulled from the wrong end.

Step 3 - Side panel removal

  1. Remove four T15 screws: two together, plus one further round the side.
  2. Pull the panel free and set it aside.
  3. Unclip the retaining clip that holds the next section down.
  4. Pop the trim piece out.
  5. Remove one T20 screw.
  6. Push the panel to release the remaining internal clips, then lift it up and out.

Step 4 - You're at the rear SAM

With that trim out, you have everything you need to install the emulator. For reference, the fuse module sits on top, the CAN gateway (what the video refers to as "the canvas") is in the main body of the unit, the roof module is underneath, and there are a couple of sensors mounted nearby.

Part 2: Wiring the Emulator

The emulator has four connections: earth, permanent 12V, and the two CAN bus wires.

Loosen the SAM housing

  1. Using the 4mm Allen key, loosen (not fully remove) the bolts holding the SAM housing - just enough that the unit can move.
  2. Remove the housing screws (T20 and T15) and lift the housing to access the connectors underneath.
  3. The unit lifts up and slides out of two slots at the back. Take your time here - there's an airbag-related loom attached, so don't pull hard.

Earth

Connect the emulator's black earth wire to a solid earth point using the 10mm socket/spanner.

12V permanent live

  1. Identify the permanent-live feed running into the rear SAM's fuse box (green/white wire in the video, coming from the consumer battery).
  2. Fit the supplied fuse tap into that fuse position - this taps the circuit without altering the car's original wiring.
  3. Connect the emulator's 12V (green/black) wire to the fuse tap.
  4. The emulator has its own internal fuse (5A, supplied fitted) - you don't need to add separate fusing beyond the tap itself.

Once earth and 12V are both connected, the emulator's LED will light solid red - powered up and waiting for a CAN signal.

CAN High / CAN Low

  1. Find the spare connector strip that used to feed the original BCM - it should already be disconnected with nothing on the end of it.
  2. The two wires you need are colour-coded red (top) and brown (bottom) at every position along that strip - red is CAN High, brown is CAN Low. Brown is not an earth - treat both as signal wires.
  3. Use a DuPont pin connector on the emulator's CAN leads to plug straight into that strip - no need to track down the original Mercedes-specific connector.
  4. Match polarity carefully: red to red (top), black/brown to brown (bottom). Push firmly - the pins are a locking fit.

Once connected, the LED changes from solid to flashing - this means the CAN bus is active and the emulator is receiving traffic.

Part 3: Testing

  1. Insert the key and start the engine.
  2. Confirm there's no battery/BCM fault warning on the dash.
  3. With the key out, watch the LED: it should keep flashing for a while, then pause and restart as the car's electronics attempt to go to sleep and get woken again by another module. This is normal R230 behaviour and not caused by the emulator itself.

A look ahead

Later firmware builds add the ability to identify which module is keeping the car's CAN bus awake (useful for chasing parasitic battery drain) and to host a small status web page from the emulator itself. These features are still in active development.

Part 4: Mounting & Reassembly

Where to put it

We recommend mounting the emulator inside the cubby space you've just opened up, so the LED is visible through the gap and the unit stays easy to reach if you ever need to unplug it. The case has mounting lugs, which are handy if you're fixing it to a flat panel - if you're tucking it into a tighter gap, you can trim the lugs off and use a strip of Velcro instead.

Reassembly - reverse of Part 1

  1. Refit the SAM housing and tighten the Allen bolts just enough to hold it - no need to overtighten.
  2. Refit the housing screws (T20/T15).
  3. Refit the side panel: line up the locating holes, seat it firmly, then refit its screws (T20).
  4. Watch for the small retaining clip that holds this trim section on - it's commonly damaged or missed. Make sure it's engaged before moving on.
  5. Refit the sill/door seal. There's a small tab on the underside that needs to locate into the edge of the carpet - if that tab or its clips have gone soft with age, a strip of tape behind them can restore enough grip to hold it firmly in place.
  6. Refit the rear center console and its two T15 screws.
