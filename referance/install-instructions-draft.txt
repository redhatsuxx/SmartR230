PAGE HEADER

Title: Install Instructions
Subtitle: Fitting the SmartR230 emulator adjacent to the rear SAM
located behind the seats


OVERVIEW
--------
This guide is the written companion to our installation walkthrough
video and covers fitting the SmartR230 emulator adjacent to the rear
SAM. It assumes the original BCM has already been removed as part of
your replacement, leaving a spare connector at the CAN bus strip that
previously fed it.

The install uses a fuse-tap method for power, so you don't need to cut
into any of the car's original wiring. Everything is reversible.

Where to put it
  We recommend mounting the emulator inside the cubby space behind the
  seats, adjacent to the rear fuse block, so the LED is visible
  through the gap and the unit stays easy to reach if you ever need to
  unplug it. The case has mounting lugs, which are handy if you're
  fixing it to a flat panel - if you're tucking it into a tighter gap,
  you can trim the lugs off and use a strip of Velcro instead.

  [callout - "Before you start"]
  Trim clips get brittle in cold weather, so this job is easier in
  mild conditions. Work carefully around the air lines and wiring loom
  near the rear SAM, and avoid yanking on connectors.


TOOLS REQUIRED
--------------
- T15 Torx - Rear console and side panel screws
- T20 Torx - Side panel and trim screws
- 10mm socket or spanner - Earth connection
- 4mm Allen key - Loosens compartment bolts so the unit can be moved
  to access the rear SAM and fuse block


PART 1: ACCESSING THE REAR SAM
-------------------------------

Step 1 - Rear center console
  1. Remove the two T15 screws inside the rear center console.
  2. Lift the centre rear console section out.

Step 2 - Clear the way
  1. Slide the driver's seat fully forward.
  2. Lift the door/sill seal off - start from the back end, since the
     front has a hook that tucks under the carpet and can tear it if
     pulled from the wrong end.

Step 3 - Metal side panel removal
  1. Remove four T15 screws: two together, plus one further round the
     side.
  2. Pull the inner panel free and set it aside.
  3. Unclip the retaining clip that holds the next section down.
  4. Pop the trim piece out.
  5. Remove one T20 screw.
  6. Push the panel to release the remaining internal clips, then lift
     it up and out.

Step 4 - Loosen the rear SAM housing
  1. Using the 4mm Allen key, loosen (not fully remove) the bolts
     holding the compartment - just enough that the unit can move.
  2. Remove the housing screws (T20 and T15) and lift the housing to
     access the connectors underneath.
  3. The unit lifts up and slides out of two slots at the back. Take
     your time here - there's an air line loom attached, so don't pull
     hard.

Step 5 - You're at the rear SAM
  With that trim out, you have everything you need to install the
  emulator. For reference, the fuse module sits on top, the CAN
  gateway is adjacent to the main body of the unit, the roof module is
  underneath, and there are a couple of sensors mounted nearby.


PART 2: WIRING THE EMULATOR
----------------------------
The emulator has three connections: earth, permanent 12V, and the two
CAN bus wires into a single dual DuPont connector.

Earth
  Connect the emulator's black earth wire to a solid earth point using
  the 10mm socket/spanner. This point is located just inside the door,
  now exposed since the trim has been removed.

12V permanent live
  1. Identify the permanent-live feed running from the rear SAM's fuse
     box (thick red/green wire from the purple fuse block).
  2. Fit the supplied fuse tap into that fuse position - this taps the
     circuit without altering the car's original wiring.
  3. The emulator has its own internal fuse (500mA, supplied fitted) -
     you don't need to add separate fusing beyond the tap itself.

  Once earth and 12V are both connected, the emulator's LED will light
  solid red - powered up and waiting for a CAN signal.

CAN High / CAN Low
  1. Find the spare connector strip that used to feed the original BCM
     - it should already be disconnected with nothing on the end of
     it.
  2. The two wires you need are colour-coded red (top) and brown
     (bottom) at every position along that strip - red is CAN High,
     brown is CAN Low. Brown is NOT an earth - treat both as signal
     wires.
  3. Use the DuPont pin connector on the emulator's CAN leads to plug
     straight into that strip; any position on the strip is fine.
  4. Important: red to red (top), black/brown to brown (bottom). Push
     firmly.

  Once connected, the LED changes from solid to flashing - this means
  the CAN bus is active and the emulator is receiving traffic. If it
  doesn't, the car may be asleep; try waking it by opening and closing
  a door. If the emulator is connected correctly, the light will
  flash.


PART 3: TESTING
----------------
  1. Insert the key and start the engine.
  2. Confirm there's no battery/BCM fault warning on the dash.
  3. With the key out, watch the LED: it should keep flashing for
     circa 20 seconds, then go steady - this is an indication the car
     has gone to sleep.


PART 4: REASSEMBLY
-------------------

Reverse of Part 1
  1. Refit the SAM housing and tighten the Allen bolts just enough to
     hold it - no need to overtighten.
  2. Refit the housing screws (T20/T15).
  3. Refit the side panel: line up the locating holes, seat it firmly,
     then refit its screws (T20).
  4. Watch for the small retaining clip that holds this trim section
     on - it's commonly damaged or missed. Make sure it's engaged
     before moving on.
  5. Refit the sill/door seal. There's a small tab on the underside
     that needs to locate into the edge of the carpet - if that tab or
     its clips have gone soft with age, a strip of tape behind them
     can restore enough grip to hold it firmly in place.
  6. Refit the rear center console and its two T15 screws.
